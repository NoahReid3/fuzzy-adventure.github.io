The repeating stages of hazard work: mitigation, preparedness, response, and recovery, often shown as a cycle, disaster management cycle.
A hazard driven or made worse by climate, e.g. drought, storm, or sea-level rise, climate-related hazard.
A hazard from Earth’s plates, e.g. earthquake, volcano, or tsunami, tectonic hazard.
A place exposed to several different hazard types at once, a multi-hazard environment.
Managing hazards so needs of people today are met without harming future generations’ ability to cope, sustainability in hazard management.
The process of improving people’s well-being and quality of life, including economic and social progress, development.
Growth in income, jobs, and output, often measured by rising GDP or GNI per person, economic development.
Improvements in health, education, equality, and rights, not only money, social development.
The total value of goods and services produced in a country in a year, GDP.
The total income earned by a country’s people and firms (including from abroad), often per person, GNI.
A composite index using life expectancy, education, and income to compare well-being between countries, HDI.
The % of adults who can read and write, literacy rate.
The average years a newborn is expected to live if current death rates continue, life expectancy (development context).
Deaths of infants under one per 1,000 live births, a key health indicator, infant mortality rate (development).
The level of material comfort, income, and services people can access, standard of living.
A person’s overall well-being, including health, environment, and happiness, not only income, quality of life.
Uneven distribution of wealth, opportunity, or outcomes between people or places, inequality.
A lack of resources to meet basic needs, often measured by an income or multi-dimensional line, poverty.
Poverty defined by a fixed minimum income or consumption line, below which needs are not met, absolute poverty.
Poverty compared to the average or typical living standard in a society, relative poverty.
A measurable figure used to describe development, e.g. GNI per capita or life expectancy, development indicator.
Economic activities that extract raw materials, e.g. farming, fishing, mining, primary sector.
Manufacturing and construction that process materials into goods, secondary sector.
Services such as retail, health, education, and finance, tertiary sector.
High-level information, research, and technology services, quaternary sector.
The growth of factory industry and manufacturing in a country or region, industrialisation.
Growing links between countries through trade, investment, migration, and media, globalisation.
A large firm that operates in many countries with production and sales across borders, TNC.
Money invested in a country from abroad, e.g. building factories, foreign direct investment.
Money, goods, or technical help given by one country or organisation to support development, aid in development.
Money owed by a government or firm that must be repaid with interest, debt.
Buying and selling goods and services between places or countries, trade.
Goods or services sold to other countries, exports.
Goods or services bought from other countries, imports.
The value of exports minus imports; can be surplus or deficit, trade balance.
Trade that pays farmers and workers fair prices and supports social and environmental standards, fair trade.
Development that meets present needs without harming future generations’ ability to meet theirs, sustainable development.
A natural resource replaced as fast as it is used, e.g. timber from well-managed forest, renewable resource.
A resource formed so slowly that it is effectively finite for human use, e.g. oil, coal, non-renewable resource.
The mix of energy sources a place uses, e.g. coal, gas, renewables, energy mix.
Travel away from home for leisure, business, or culture, often a key industry, tourism.
Very large numbers of visitors to a place, with possible pressure on the environment, mass tourism.
Responsible travel that conserves nature and supports local people, ecotourism.
Jobs, income, and foreign exchange from visitors, economic impact of tourism.
Habitat loss, pollution, and footpath erosion from visitors, environmental impact of tourism.
Changes in culture, behaviour, and community life from contact with tourists, social impact of tourism.
Planning use and protection of soils, water, forests, and fish so they last, resource management.
When demand for water exceeds reliable supply in an area, water scarcity.
When supply of water comfortably meets needs, water surplus.
When not enough food is available or affordable for people to be healthy, food shortage.
Catching fish faster than stocks can replace themselves, overfishing.
Clearing forest for farms, timber, or roads, often reducing biodiversity, deforestation.
Dryland becoming desert through climate and human pressure, often with soil loss, desertification.
The growth of towns and cities and the share of people living in them, urbanisation in development.
Movement of people seeking work or safety, affecting source and host development, migration in development.
The gap in income and well-being between the richest and poorest countries or people, development gap.
A country with low GNI per capita in World Bank terms, often with limited industry, LIC.
A country between LIC and HIC by income, MIC.
A country with high GNI per capita; very developed economy, HIC.
Technology suited to local skills, culture, and environment, often small-scale, appropriate technology.
Simple, affordable technology between traditional and high-tech, improving lives (e.g. fair trade craft), intermediate technology.
Small loans to people who cannot borrow from banks, often for micro-business, microfinance loan.
Basic systems such as roads, power, water, schools, and communications, infrastructure.
The delivery of electricity, gas, and other energy to homes and firms, energy supply.
When all people have reliable access to enough safe, nutritious food, food security.
When people have reliable access to safe, affordable water, water security.
When a country relies too much on aid and cannot fund its own services, aid dependency.
A non-profit group working on development or environment, independent of government, NGO.
Aid given directly by one government to another, government or bilateral-style aid.
Aid from one country’s government to another’s, bilateral aid.
Aid channelled through international bodies (e.g. World Bank) for several countries, multilateral aid.
Rapid help after disaster (food, shelter, medicine), emergency aid.
Aid for years of projects in health, education, and infrastructure, long-term aid.
Rules or taxes that limit imports or exports, trade barrier.
A tax on imported goods, making them more expensive, tariff.
A limit on how much of a good may be imported, quota.
A policy to replace imports by producing goods at home, import substitution.
Growing the economy by selling more goods and services abroad, export-led growth.
A treaty between countries to reduce barriers to trade between them, free trade agreement.
Reducing dependence on one product or sector by growing new industries, economic diversification.
Using up resources faster than they can be replaced, resource depletion.
Using the environment in a way that keeps ecosystems healthy for the future, environmental sustainability.
Release of CO₂ and other greenhouse gases, often from burning fossil fuels, carbon emissions.
Building more wind, solar, and other renewable capacity, renewable energy development.
Rules and practices that balance use and conservation of soil, water, forests, and fisheries, sustainable resource management.
